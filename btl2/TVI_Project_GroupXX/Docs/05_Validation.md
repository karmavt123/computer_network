### How to read the outages

* **About 9 s** wherever the *far end* of a tunnel has to notice the failure: nothing but the BGP hold timer (3 s keepalive / 9 s hold) tells a hub that a spoke's uplink died, or a spoke that the hub's did.
* **About 4 s** when traffic was transiting a router that died silently: OSPF dead interval.
* **Under 2 s** for a gateway failure: HSRP hold time 1.5 s.
* **About 1 s** when the failing interface belongs to the router that has to react: it sees its own link go down and the track object follows at the next 1 s poll. IOS is slower here than FRR (BGP next-hop trigger delay of 5 s) - expect a few seconds in the lab.

## 4. Residual risks (known, accepted)

* **Double failure** of the HQ edge-to-edge link *and* a branch tunnel on the primary ISP re-creates the loop (S9). Mitigations for production: a second edge-to-edge link in a port-channel, or iBGP on the core switches.
* **One access switch per branch** in the lab: its failure isolates that branch's hosts.
* **Detection bound of 9 s** on tunnels; BFD is the roadmap item.
* **A dead ISP router that keeps its customer link up** is detected by BGP hold (9 s), not by link state.
